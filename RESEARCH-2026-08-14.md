# ORB Literature Review vs. This Bot — 2026-08-14

Source review + gap analysis, written after the 60-trade paper trial came in at
net +4.67 (t = 0.03). Read §1 before §3.

---

## 1. The funding gate has to change first

The proposed trigger is "one fairly profitable week, then fund it." Simulated
against this trial's own per-trade distribution, re-centred to **zero edge**
(200,000 draws, per-trade sd = 20.64, ~11 trades/week):

| trades in the week | P(week is profitable \| NO edge) |
|---|---|
| 5 | 46.1% |
| 11 | 47.4% |
| 15 | 47.9% |

A profitable week is a coin flip. It is not evidence. And because the plan is to
tune on failure and re-test, the gate gets passed by repetition:

| tune-and-retest attempts | P(at least one profitable week) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 47% |
| 2 | 72% |
| 3 | 85% |
| 4 | 92% |
| 6 | 98% |

Following this process to its conclusion funds the bot with ~99% probability
**whether or not it has any edge whatsoever.** The gate cannot fail, so it
carries no information. It is not a safeguard; it is a formality that feels like
one.

What sample size would actually be informative, at 80% power:

| true edge | trades needed | ≈ weeks |
|---|---|---|
| +1 / trade | 3,340 | 304 |
| +2 / trade | 835 | 76 |
| +5 / trade | 134 | 12 |

**Implication:** only a large edge is detectable on any realistic timeline. The
goal is not "make the bot slightly better." It is "find out whether a version
exists with an effect big enough to see." If the answer is +1/trade, the honest
conclusion is that this cannot be validated with a small account and should not
be funded on live results.

## 2. The sources

**Primary.** Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz (2024), *A Profitable Day Trading Strategy
For The U.S. Equity Market*, Swiss Finance Institute RP 24-98. 7,000+ US stocks,
2016–2023, CRSP + IQFeed, survivorship-bias free.

**Companion.** Zarattini & Aziz (2023/2025), QQQ/TQQQ ORB. Different rule
(entry at 9:35 on first-candle direction, not a breakout) — not what this bot does.

**Antecedents.** Crabel (1990) — original ORB. Tsai et al. (2019) — profits
concentrate within 5 minutes of the open. Wu et al. (2020) — **profit targets
were detrimental; stop-losses helped.** Lundström (2017) — ORB profitability
rises with volatility.

### The result that matters most

The paper's base 5-min ORB across all liquid US stocks:

| | Base ORB | + RelVol filter | S&P 500 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total return (8y) | **29%** | **1,637%** | 198% |
| Annualized | 3.2% | 41.6% | 14.2% |
| Sharpe | 0.48 | 2.81 | 0.78 |
| **Hit ratio** | **41.4%** | 48.4% | — |
| Max drawdown | 13% | 12% | 34% |

**This bot's hit ratio is 41.7%.** That is the base-strategy number to within
0.3 points. The bot is a faithful, working implementation of the variant the
paper found does not work. That is the diagnosis: not a broken bot, but the
wrong ORB.

Relative volume is where the edge lives (paper Fig. 4, avg PnL in R):

| opening relative volume | avg PnL/trade |
|---|---|
| < 100% | **−0.02R** |
| > 100% | +0.08R |
| > 3,000% | **+0.38R** |

And opening-range length, all with the RelVol filter on:

| OR length | Total return | Sharpe | Max DD |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5 min** | **1,637%** | **2.81** | 12% |
| 15 min | 272% | 1.43 | 11% |
| 30 min | 21% | 0.21 | 35% |
| 60 min | 39% | 0.40 | 21% |

## 3. Where this bot diverges from the profitable variant

Ranked by the effect size the paper reports.

| # | Paper | `config.yaml` now | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5-min opening range | `opening_range_minutes: 15` | 5m→15m costs ~6x return, Sharpe 2.81→1.43 |
| 2 | RelVol ≥ 100%, hard filter | `min_opening_rel_volume: 0.0` (measure-only) | This filter *is* the edge |
| 3 | Rank by opening RelVol, top 20 | ranks by gap% + news sentiment | Selecting on the wrong variable |
| 4 | Direction = first 5-min candle | `respect_news_bias`, sentiment ≥ 0.50 | News-sentiment bias is **not in the literature at all** |
| 5 | Stop = 10% of 14-day ATR | `stop_pct: 1.5` flat | Volatility-scaled vs fixed; `--atr-stop` exists |
| 6 | Exit: stop or EOD. No trail, no TP | `trail_pct: 1.25` | Paper never trails. Wu (2020): targets hurt |
| 7 | ATR14 > $0.50 required | no ATR floor | Bot can trade names with no range to capture |
| 8 | Entry on 5-min range break (early) | `entry_window_minutes: 180` | Live data: entries ≥60min = **−122.50 over 12 trades** |

Item 8 was found independently in this trial's own data before reading the
paper, and it agrees with the paper's design. That convergence is the strongest
single signal in this document.

## 4. Two blockers that no amount of tuning fixes

### 4.1 The IEX feed cannot measure relative volume

`runtime.data_feed: iex`. IEX is ~4% of consolidated tape — the config already
compensates by scaling the liquidity floor ~/25. Relative volume over a 5-minute
window on 4% of the tape is a very small, very noisy sample.

Evidence from 2026-08-13: `RELVOL CLBT: 110051 vs 857 avg -> 128.37x`. An
857-share baseline is not a measurement. A RelVol gate calibrated on numbers
like that will fire on noise.

**The single highest-value change from the literature is the one the current
data feed cannot support.** Implementing it properly needs a paid consolidated
(SIP) feed — check current Alpaca pricing.

### 4.2 The account cannot hold the paper's portfolio

The paper's 2.81 Sharpe comes from **20 simultaneous positions, long and short,
up to 4x leverage**, rebalanced daily. This account is:

- small account, **cash** (no margin, no leverage)
- `allow_shorts: false` — and the paper's trades are roughly half short
- `max_position_notional: 700` → about 2–3 concurrent positions

So even a perfect implementation cannot reproduce the paper's result, because
the diversification across 20 names is what produces the Sharpe. A 2-position
long-only fragment of a 20-position long/short portfolio has dramatically higher
variance and a materially different return distribution. This is a structural
mismatch between the strategy and the account, not a tuning problem.

## 5. Caveats on the paper itself

Worth holding in mind before betting on it:

- **No clearly separated out-of-sample period.** The authors argue parameters
  came from "economic rationale rather than retrospective optimization," but
  there is no post-selection validation window in the paper.
- **Simplified execution.** $0.0035/share commission; no spread, no slippage on
  stop fills, no short-locate costs, no halts. Event-driven stocks — exactly the
  ones the filter selects — have the worst spreads. Cf. NEBX on 08-13: 3.46%
  spread, entered anyway, straight to a stop.
- **Published February 2024; it is now August 2026.** No independent
  out-of-sample replication found. Two and a half years of public availability.
- **Concentration risk is real:** the filtered portfolio's worst day was *worse*
  than the base strategy's (−1.61% vs −0.8%).

## 6. Recommended order of work

Nothing here touches `config.yaml` until the backtest says so.

**Step 1 — backtest the two big levers.** Must run on your machine; the sandbox
is firewalled off from Alpaca.

```
python sweep.py --start 2026-06-08 --end 2026-08-08 \
    --or-minutes 5,15,30 --stops 1.5 --bias 0.5 \
    --trail 1.25,off --arm breakeven --tp off --no-shorts
```

```
python sweep.py --start 2026-06-08 --end 2026-08-08 \
    --or-minutes 5 --relvol off,1.0,1.5,2.0 --stops 1.5 \
    --bias 0.5,off --trail off --tp off --no-shorts
```

Note `--relvol` in sim uses a **daily** proxy (prev-day vol / 20d avg), not the
paper's opening-window metric. Directional only; do not treat as validation.

**Step 2 — add `--entry-window` to `sweep.py`.** The biggest effect in the live
data currently has no sweep dimension. Highest value per hour of work available.

**Step 3 — extend the backtest window.** Two months is not enough to separate
in- and out-of-sample. Pull 2024–2026 and hold the last 6 months back, untouched,
until the ruleset is frozen.

**Step 4 — decide on the data feed** before building anything on RelVol.

**Step 5 — pre-commit the gate.** Fix the ruleset, write down the number of
trades and the criterion *in advance*, run it, evaluate once. No mid-flight
tuning. If it fails, that is a real answer.

---

## 7. Bottom line

The bot is not broken; it is a correct implementation of the ORB variant the
literature says returns ~3%/year. The published fix — 5-minute range, relative-
volume selection — is real, sizeable, and specific.

But two things stand between this bot and that result: a data feed that cannot
measure the key variable, and an account that cannot hold the portfolio the
result depends on. Both cost money to fix, and neither is addressed by tuning
parameters.

The most useful thing available right now is Step 1 plus Step 3: find out
whether the 5-minute range and a RelVol filter produce a *large* effect on your
own data, out of sample. A large effect can be validated in about 12 weeks. A
small one cannot be validated at all on this account, and that is itself a
decision-relevant finding.

Nothing here is financial advice, and I'm not a financial adviser — this is an
analysis of the code, the trial data, and the published research.
