# Screener diversity audit — 2026-10-15

Trigger: "We need a larger diversity in the stocks the bot buys. It buys almost
the same ones consistently." Measured over 59 session logs and 130 booked paper
trades (local copies synced through 2026-10-15).

**Read `exit-structure.md` first.** Its meta-lesson governs this file: *this
dataset can speak to exits, it cannot speak to selection*. Two of the three
changes below are mechanism fixes that do not claim P&L. The third IS a
selection claim, it contradicts nothing but it is unvalidated, and it is
flagged as such.

## 1. The finding: the candidate pool collapsed to a median of 6

| | value |
|---|---|
| `max_candidates` cap | 40 |
| candidates actually kept, median/session | **6** |
| sessions keeping <= 8 | 41 of 59 |
| names rejected per session (liquidity) | 46 - 63 |

Recent sessions kept 1 (10-06), 2 (10-08), 2 (10-12), 6 (10-15). `max_targets`
is 10 and has NEVER been the binding constraint. The pool is.

Concentration that follows from it — targeted in N of 59 sessions:
INTC 30, SOXS 24, SNXX 23, SOXL 21, NOK 19, SPCX 15, TSLL 13.
Top 8 names = 64 of 130 trades (**49%**). 46 names traded exactly once.

Since 09-18, six repeat names took 27 of 42 trades and every one is net
negative: SOXL -21.73, SOXS -27.46, INTC -15.97, SNXX -24.85, NOK -22.02,
SPCX -8.96 (**-121 combined**). Per the meta-lesson this is a subset finding
and is NOT offered as evidence of an edge.

## 2. Root cause A — a filter that never fired (BUG, not a hypothesis)

`screener.py: is_common_stock_symbol()` only tested DOTTED suffixes
(`NVA.WS`). Alpaca returns NASDAQ fifth-letter instruments UNDOTTED: `DAICW`,
`ASTLW`, `OPENW`, `SEATW` (warrants), `HUBCZ`, `AMPGZ` (misc), `HCICR`,
`IRHOR` (rights). **The check never fired on any of them.**

Of 2,306 illiquid rejections across the 59 logs, **1,422 (62%) end in
W/R/U/Z.** DAICW was rejected 31 separate times, ASTLW 26, ARBEW 16.

Why that shrinks the pool: the feed pulls are capped
(`top_most_actives` + `top_movers`), and warrants make enormous percentage
moves, so they rank high in "top movers" and **displaced real tradeable names
inside the pull, before the liquidity floor ever ran.** The floor then swept
them out, leaving ~6 real names. The 40-slot `max_candidates` cut was never
reached — so this was never a cap problem.

**Fix:** length-pinned fifth-letter check (exactly 5 chars ending W/R/U/Z).
Verified against the actual rejected tickers: all 12 junk tickers filtered,
all 23 real names kept (SNOW, WOLF, FNGU, SOXL, GOOGL, BRK.B, AAAU, UDOW...).
Four-letter names are untouched by construction. New `NON-COMMON skipped` log
line makes the pool auditable per session.

## 3. Root cause B — the liquidity floor was a large-cap gate

`min_prev_day_volume: 2_000_000` consolidated shares, against a $450 position.
What cleared that AND `$5-400` AND `>=2% gap` was a structurally tiny set: 3x
ETFs plus a few megacaps. Hence the same names daily.

Lowered to **500,000**. The incident that motivated the floor (AHCO 08-04/05,
unfillable exit) is guarded at ENTRY by `max_spread_pct: 0.5`, which is the
check that actually addresses fill quality. The sticky same-day illiquid set
is unchanged. Also raised `top_most_actives` 20 -> 50, `top_movers` 20 -> 50,
`max_candidates` 40 -> 60, now that warrants no longer consume the pull.

## 4. Root cause C — leveraged ETPs bypass every directional filter

`gap_bias_min_pct: 0`, so only news sets a bias. **All nine SOXS trades logged
"0 articles"** -> sentiment 0.00 -> bias `any` on every one. So the v1.9
dead-slot skip never drops it and `respect_news_bias` never blocks it: the bot
takes whichever way the opening range breaks. It went long SOXS on gap-up days
(+11.1%, +9.8%, +5.0%) and gap-down days (-7.5%, -4.8%) alike.

SOXS, all 9 trades: 4 wins (+3.85, +4.12, +12.32, +1.40), 5 losses
(-10.27, -10.96, -10.12, -10.29, -10.35), **net -30.30**. Every loss is the
full stop; the wins were truncated at +0.20% to +1.81%. MFE shows 7 of 9 were
green at some point, two past +1.4%.

Added `selection.cap_leveraged_etps` + `max_leveraged_etps: 1`, reusing the
existing `_LEVERAGED_NAME` detector.

### ⚠️ SHIPPED OFF (`max_leveraged_etps: 0`) — selection claim, NOT supported

Measured on the actual booked trades, `max_leveraged_etps=1` would have:

- dropped **12 trades** across 10 sessions, total PnL **-1.80**
- changed the negative-day count by **zero** (28/47 before, 28/47 after)

That is consistent with the already-rejected hypothesis *"crypto / leveraged
worse — all die ex-top2"*. It is also an unpre-registered strategy parameter,
which would break the paper/live comparability rule in `config-live.yaml`.

**Decision 2026-10-15: shipped with `max_leveraged_etps: 0`.** The code and
tests are in place; the knob is off. It is a risk-concentration control, not
an edge, and turning it on needs pre-committed criteria first. So **nothing in
this deploy changes entry or exit logic** — paper/live comparability and the
trial's denomination are both intact, and the only behavioural change is WHICH
names reach the target list.

# §9 PRE-REGISTRATION — stop 1.0 + trail, forward test

**Written 2026-10-15 BEFORE running `replay.py`. Nothing below was chosen after
seeing a result.** This is the test `exit-structure.md` reserved: *"The only
remaining test is forward paper data with pre-registered criteria — there is
nothing left to backtest it on."*

## Why now

| | 2026-10-01 (in-sample) | 2026-10-15 |
|---|---|---|
| trades in `--since 2026-08-19` | 38 | **70** |
| of which generated AFTER the test | — | **30 (out-of-sample)** |

The 30 post-10-01 trades were never seen when `stop 1.0 + trail` was selected,
so they are a real holdout. The prior in-sample result, touch mode, was
**+17.80 full / +17.81 ex-top2 / +17.80 ex-top3**, with the stated mechanism
*"its gain does not come from the winners at all — it comes from losing less
on the trades that fail."*

## The command

```powershell
python -u replay.py --robust --since 2026-08-19
```

Also run the holdout slice on its own:

```powershell
python -u replay.py --robust --since 2026-10-02
```

`touch` is the correct fill mode (validated 10-01: replay -4.02 vs actual
-4.66 across 38 trades). **`close` is reported but is NOT the decision mode.**

## Pre-committed criteria — all four must hold

Judged on the **n=30 holdout** (`--since 2026-10-02`), in **touch** mode:

1. **Sign.** Delta vs LIVE is **positive**.
2. **Not a tail.** Delta stays positive **ex-top2**. The claimed mechanism is
   losing less on losers, so dropping the best two should barely move it —
   in-sample it moved by 0.01. A delta that collapses ex-top2 **refutes the
   stated mechanism**, not just the magnitude.
3. **Stability.** Both halves of the holdout carry the **same sign**.
4. **Consistency.** The full n=70 run agrees in sign with the holdout.

## Declared in advance

- **If all four hold:** it is still not significant at n=30. It earns a
  pre-registered LIVE trial at current sizing, not a claim of edge.
- **If criterion 2 fails:** `stop 1.0 + trail` joins the rejected list. It
  would be the **11th rejection**, and it dies the same way NO-TRAIL did.
- **If 1, 3 or 4 fail:** unresolved, n too small. **Do not re-test it on a
  different `--since` date** — that is the subset trap with a date instead of
  a bucket, already flagged against ourselves on 10-01.
- A 1.0% stop means MORE stop-outs. That was a caveat in-sample and remains
  one; it is the opposite direction from volatility-normalisation.
- **No tuning.** The grid is not reopened on this data. One candidate, four
  criteria, declared above.

## 10.5 Three things that keep this from being a result

1. **t = +0.44 to +0.61.** Nowhere near significant. n=30.

3. **The harness's own gate is unmet.** It prints *"Read `eod` first. If those
   do not reproduce, fix that before believing anything about stops."* `eod` is
   the worst category (n=4, median err 1.16%, 2 of 4 past 0.5%). Stops

## 10.6 Recommendation — do NOT adopt yet

§9 declared that passing "earns a pre-registered LIVE trial at current sizing,
not a claim of edge." Holding to that would mean adopting stop 1.0. **I am

identically-shaped tests, adopting either is a pick from five on n=30, and
`config-live.yaml`'s rule means the change lands in paper AND live together —

What would actually settle it, in order:

1. **Fix the `eod` reproduction** (n=4, median 1.16%). It is the harness's own
   stated precondition and it is four trades' worth of work to diagnose.
2. **Separate the two candidates.** They differ mechanically: one tightens the

   A test that distinguishes them beats a bake-off on total delta.
3. **Let the holdout grow.** n=30 now, and the bot adds ~2-3 trades a session.
   Re-score at n=60 with these same four criteria and no new ones.

**Do not re-run on a different `--since` date, and do not reopen the grid.**

# §11 THE HARNESS BUG — found, fixed, verified. §10 IS NOW VOID.

The +96.68 validation gap was **not** structural. `--per-trade` localized it to
four trades, and the dollar sum of its printed errors came to **+111.01** — which
reconciled to the CLOSE-mode gap (+111.00), not touch (+96.68). That mismatch
was the first clue: **`per_trade()` hardcoded `mode="close"`** while `touch` is
the decision mode. Fixed; it now takes the mode and prints it in the header.

## Two real gaps, both in the harness, neither in the strategy

**1. `exit_on_adverse` was not modelled at all.** `config.yaml` has
`exit_on_adverse: true`, and the bot closes a position on a fresh adverse
headline. The replay had no model for it, so those trades were held to the
15:50 flatten and credited with range they never got:

| trade | log | actual exit | replay held to | err |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NKE 10-02 | `12:24:53 ADVERSE NEWS EXIT (score -1.00)` | 33.18 | 33.845 | +2.00% |
| INTC 10-13 | `10:22:02 ADVERSE NEWS EXIT (score -1.00)` | 100.41 | 102.39 | +1.96% |

Unmodellable from bars — the trigger is the news feed, not price — but the bot
logs it as a timestamped WARNING. Added `_adverse_exits_from_logs()`, sharing
the entry reader's CEST/ET self-calibration, a `news` exit in `replay()`
checked after stop/trail (those are resting orders and fill first), and the
`news` kind in `actual_exit_types()` — it had been falling through to the
`pnl_pct <= -1.35` fallback and being labelled `eod`, so the harness was
"agreeing with itself about the wrong exit rule."

**2. WFF 10-12 was a circuit-breaker halt artifact, and it is 71% of the gap.**

```
10:06  TRAIL ACTIVATED: peak +9.97%, armed at +9.97% (locks ~+8.60%)
10:16  TRAIL EXIT: trailing stop filled (peak was +9.97%)
       Halted On Circuit Breaker ... Up 92% / 114% / 150% / 240%
15:50  21.46 -> 27.19   PnL +183.42 (+26.71%)
```

**The trail armed at +9.97% and would have locked ~+8.60%. It filled at
+26.71%** — the resting stop sat through repeated halts and filled on a
re-open gap far above its level. No bar-walking replay can reproduce that, and
it is not a property of any exit rule. Added `has_trading_halt()` (a gap of
>=2 minutes in the minute stream with a >=3% jump across it); such trades now
return `None, "halted"` and are EXCLUDED, the same treatment already given to
no-bars. **It flags exactly one trade in the 70-trade window: WFF.**

## 12.2 ⛔ CORRECTION — the `t` column does not mean what §10.5.1 said

`robust()` line 50 computes `t` from **the candidate's own per-trade P&L**, not
from the delta vs LIVE. It answers *"does this strategy's P&L differ from
zero"*, not *"is this improvement real"*. Hence LIVE printing `t=-6.22` on a
delta of identically +0.00.

§10.5.1 read it as a significance test of the improvement and called it
"nowhere near significant". **That was wrong.** The deltas have never been
significance-tested at all, in this run or any earlier one. Fixing `t` to test
the delta series is now a prerequisite for any adoption decision.

What the column DOES say, read correctly: every candidate's P&L is reliably
negative. `stop 1.0 + trail` posts t=-6.36.

## 12.4 Two reasons the test has lost its power to discriminate

**1. Four of five candidates now beat LIVE, and THREE pass all four criteria**
(`trail 0.75 arm 0.75`, `stop 1.0 + trail`, `stop 1.0 NO TRAIL`). When almost
every alternative beats the incumbent, the informative conclusion is about the
incumbent, not about which alternative to pick. Choosing the biggest number
among three passers on n=29 is the subset trap with a candidate list instead
of a date.

**2. ⚠️ My halt-exclusion is NOT candidate-neutral, and it favours NO-TRAIL.**
WFF was a **trail** exit (+183.42) and it is the trade I removed. Taking the
trail's single best outcome out of the sample structurally disadvantages every
trail-bearing candidate — and on cue, `NO TRAIL`, **rejected twice before as
the 10th rejection**, now passes both halves on the full window, and
`stop 1.0, NO TRAIL` is the largest delta on the board.

That reversal is most likely an artifact of my own patch, not a new finding.
The exclusion is still correct — the fill was a halt gap and unmodellable —
but it is not neutral, and no conclusion should rest on it alone.
