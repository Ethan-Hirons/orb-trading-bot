# ANALYSIS 2026-10-25 — exit-rule replay, and why its results are not usable

**Nothing was deployed. `config.yaml` and `state/` untouched. No PREREG written —
the candidate hit its own abort condition.**

Started from a live question: on 2026-10-23 LKQ and TTMI appeared to sell
without reaching their stops. They did not. Everything below came out of
chasing that.

## 1. The 10-23 question — ANSWERED, no bug

| | entry | exit | stop | P&L |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LKQ | 18.1565 | 17.8851 | 17.8841 | -6.51 (-1.495%) |
| TTMI | 78.265 | 77.155 | 77.0910 | -5.55 (-1.418%) |

Both hit the -1.5% bracket stop. `-6.51 + -5.55 = -12.06`, which is the
-0.80% the equity heartbeat froze at (the account went to all cash at
10:26:55; the heartbeat then logs every 5 min by `EQUITY_LOG_INTERVAL`).
The adverse-news exit is ruled out: every `NEWS LKQ` / `NEWS TTMI` line
scored +0.00 or +1.00, nowhere near the -0.5 threshold.

**A stop-limit exit routinely fills BETTER than its trigger** (LKQ stop
17.87, filled 17.8851). So comparing a booked exit % against the configured
stop % will keep looking like "it sold early" when nothing is wrong. See §2.

## 2. REAL BUG — `_reanchor_exits` leaves most stops anchored to `ref`

`runner._reanchor_exits` re-prices the bracket to the fill only when
`|fill-ref|/ref >= 0.001`. Below that the stop keeps the level it was
submitted with, anchored to the pre-trade REFERENCE price. **98 of 158
historical trades never re-anchored.**

Consequence: the realized stop is not 1.5%. Measured across the 28
non-re-anchored trades in the replay window it spans **-1.405%
(SNXX 10-19) to -1.590% (LKQ 10-23)**.

NOK 2026-10-13 is where it decided a trade: ref 9.82, fill 9.8150, so the
stop sat at 9.6727 (-1.43% from the fill) instead of 9.6629. Price touched
9.6727, stopped out, then rallied.

**Magnitude check: mean realized stop is -1.490% vs -1.500% configured.**
A 0.01pp bias. This is a correctness and reproducibility problem, NOT a
P&L problem, and NOT an explanation for expectancy. Do not inflate it.

Fix (not yet applied): `runner.py` `drift < 0.001` -> `0.0001`. If applied,
`replay.py`'s `REANCHOR_DRIFT` needs a DATE CUTOFF at the same time, or
trades split into two anchoring regimes and the harness silently breaks
again.

## 3. `replay.py` — three fixes, harness now trustworthy

1. **Stop anchoring** (`Trade.stop_anchor`) — model the ref/fill rule above
   instead of anchoring every stop to the fill.
2. **`dump()` crash** — `t.dollars(px)` with `px=None` for unreplayable
   trades; skip before the call.
3. **`grid()` ACTUAL baseline** — summed P&L over all 35 trades while the
   rule totals covered the 34 replayable ones. The excluded trade is a
   **+93.20 winner**, biasing every `d/trade` cell by ~-2.66.

Validation after the fixes:

```
touch  replay -43.96  actual -46.95  diff +2.99   (34 of 35)
exit type correct 34/34   median err 0.02%   nothing >0.5% off
```

Residual errors are mixed in sign (+0.40 .. -0.08) = fill noise, not bias.
`close` diverges (+20.21) BY DESIGN — it only fills when a bar closes
through the level, which is not how a broker stop works. Treat close-mode
as a pessimism bound, never as a reproduction of actuals.

## 4. ⛔ THE GRID RESULTS ARE NOT USABLE — bar-resolution artifact

Every rule family's optimum sat at the SMALLEST value tested. Each time the
grid was extended downward, the optimum moved to the new edge:

| family | best cell | position |
|---|---|---|
| stop width | 0.5 | smallest tested |
| trail width | 0.25 / 0.375 | smallest tested |
| breakeven | 0.25 | smallest tested |

Three independent families cannot share an optimum by coincidence; they
share an artifact. **`replay.py` walks 1-MINUTE bars and sees only each
minute's high and low.** A 1.5% stop is far enough out that minute extremes
capture nearly every real trigger. A 0.5% stop, or a breakeven at +0.25%,
sits inside the noise the bars average away, so the replay MISSES triggers
that would really have fired — and the bias grows as the rule tightens.
That is exactly the observed shape. It also explains the non-monotonic
zigzag in the E6 grid (+0.25 best, +0.375 worse, +0.5 worse, +0.625 better).

**Conclusion: "should the bot exit sooner?" is NOT adjudicable with this
tool at any setting tighter than the current ones.** Not a robustness
problem — a measurement-resolution problem. Fixing it needs sub-minute
bars or trade prints, not more grid cells.

Compounding it: the replay fills every stop exactly AT the stop level with
zero slippage. Measured entry slippage is mean 0.046%, worst bucket 0.30%.
Against 1.5% that is noise; against 0.5% it is up to 60% of the stop
distance. Same direction, same flattering-the-tightest-rule effect.

## 5. E6 BREAKEVEN — candidate found, then ABORTED by its own rule

`BE at +0.75` passed every robustness gate — the first thing in this
project's history to do so:

```
touch  full +24.74  ex-top2 +24.74  ex-top3 +24.74  half1 +18.81  half2 +5.93  SAME SIGN
close  full +18.95  ex-top2 +18.95  ex-top3 +18.95  half1 +13.02  half2 +5.93  SAME SIGN
```

Ex-top2 and ex-top3 identical to full = concentration is not carrying it.
It also had a MECHANISM matching an independently measured defect (the
dead band, ANALYSIS-2026-09-29) and needed no new code —
`_check_breakeven()` already runs every loop.

**Pre-committed abort condition, written BEFORE the run: "if BE +0.5 or
+0.25 beats +0.75 in both modes, it is a slope not a peak — dead."**

Result: BE +0.25 gives touch +8.86 / close +10.17 against +0.75's
-19.22 / -7.79. **Beats it in both modes. ABORTED.** Per the locked rule the
holdout is NOT re-run with a different trigger. No PREREG written.

## 6. ⛔ E5 SCALE-OUT IS DEAD — do not build the partial-exit path

Worse than live at every trigger, clearly so in `close`:

```
half at +1.0   -41.05 / -44.09      half at +1.25  -52.31 / -37.39
half at +1.5   -46.17 / -33.03      half at +2.0   -44.45 / -27.52
```

(live: -43.96 / -26.74)

E5 was the RECOMMENDED next experiment out of ANALYSIS-2026-09-29. That
recommendation came from MFE summary statistics without ordering;
`replay.py` has ordering and is the better tool. This cancels the most
expensive item in the queue — Alpaca brackets are full-qty OCO and there is
no partial-exit path in `orb_bot/`.

Caveat: §4 applies here too. E5 is rejected, not disproven.

## 7. ✅ THE TRAIL EARNS ITS KEEP — settled

```
stop 1.5, NO TRAIL (paper)   -73.50 / -47.52
stop 1.5 + trail (LIVE)      -43.96 / -26.74
```

The source paper (Zarattini/Barbon/Aziz) uses no trailing stop. The bot's
trail is its own invention, validated in-sample only. On bar-level replay it
beats no-trail by ~30 points in both modes. §4's bias works AGAINST the
trail here (the trail is the tighter rule), so if anything this understates
it. Keep the trail. Question closed.

## 8. Trade record is incomplete

158 `ENTER` lines in logs vs 149 rows in `trade_history.csv`. Eight genuine
misses: 07-01 DRAM/SDOT/SSTK, 07-14 FIGG, 07-16 INTC, 07-28 MPLT,
07-29 HURN, 08-27 INTC. Cause is known and still open (decision 0):
**trades are booked at day end only**, so any mid-session stop loses them
until `backfill.py` runs.

`trade_log.xlsx` is NOT written by the bot. `live/make_trade_log.py`
regenerates it from the CSV and only runs when invoked. As of 2026-10-25
`live/trade_log.xlsx` was last written 10-12 — 11 days stale.

## 9. Standing conclusions

- **Do not re-propose**: E5 scale-out (§6); no-trail (§7); any stop, trail
  or breakeven tightening justified by `replay.py` grids (§4).
- **Do not cite** `replay.py` grid cells below the current live settings as
  evidence of anything until the resolution problem in §4 is fixed.
- The opening hypothesis — "the bot exits too early" — was not supported.
  Friday's stops were correct, and every apparent counter-result traced to a
  measurement artifact.
- t-stats across every candidate ran -1.14 to +0.64. On n=34 nothing here is
  distinguishable from zero, abort condition or not.
- What would move this forward: sub-minute exit data, or more trades.
