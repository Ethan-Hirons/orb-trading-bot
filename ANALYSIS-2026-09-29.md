# Exit-side analysis — the dead band

_2026-09-29. Read alongside `EXPERIMENTS.md`. Nothing here has been deployed.
No config, no `state/`, no `orb_bot/` file was changed to produce it._

Everything this project has tested is on the **entry** side: which names, when,
how big, what filter. `EXPERIMENTS.md` E1–E4 are all entry-side. The exit side
has been swept exactly once (Run 2: trail 1.25 vs off) and never revisited.

This is an exit-side finding. It is the first result in the project's history
that gets *stronger* when you drop the top 2 trades.

## Data

79 of the 100 baseline trades carry an MFE/MAE line in the logs and join to
`state/trade_history.csv` on (date, symbol). Those 79 total **−52.65**.

**Caveat that caps everything below:** MFE is measured off minute-bar highs,
and most of this data predates the v1.18 excursion guards. A one-print wick
high is a price you could never have sold into. Every counterfactual here is
therefore an **upper bound**, not a forecast. This is the exact failure mode
that reversed Runs 1, 5 and Track B.

## The finding: every trade gives back the same 2 points

| MFE bucket | n | mean MFE | mean booked | give-back | total $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≤ 0.5% | 15 | +0.02% | −1.24% | 1.26 pp | −127.24 |
| 0.5–1.25% | 19 | +0.88% | −1.31% | 2.18 pp | −169.10 |
| 1.25–2% | 18 | +1.51% | −0.26% | 1.77 pp | −32.47 |
| 2–3% | 7 | +2.60% | +0.25% | 2.35 pp | +9.76 |
| 3%+ | 20 | +4.40% | +2.01% | 2.40 pp | +266.40 |

By exit type: EOD flatten (n=68) gives back **2.13 pp**, time stop (n=11)
gives back **1.09 pp**.

The give-back is present in **every bucket and every exit type**. It is not a
tail artifact and not a couple of bad trades — it is the shape of the book.

## The mechanism (promotion-gate criterion 5)

The stop is 1.5%. The trail arms only when trailing 1.25% *from here* still
locks ≥ breakeven — so the position must be up **~1.25%** before the trail can
engage at all.

That leaves a **dead band between 0% and +1.25%** in which the bot holds an
open profit and has **no mechanism of any kind** to protect it. The only exits
available in that band are the −1.5% stop, the 180-minute time stop, and the
EOD flatten.

**30 of 79 trades (38%) peaked inside that dead band.** They booked a mean of
−1.23% and lost **−251.53** between them — against a whole-trial P&L of −22.60.

Put the other way: **17 trades reached +1.0% in profit and then went on to
lose, booking a mean −2.27% for a total of −265.57.** Every one of those was,
at some point, a winning trade the bot had no way to act on.

## E5 — Partial scale-out at the trail's arming threshold  ★ recommended next

**Change:** on reaching **+1.25%**, sell **half** the position at market. The
remaining half keeps its existing stop and trail, unchanged.

Why a scale-out rather than a tighter stop: selling half cannot stop you out of
the runner. The half you keep behaves exactly as it does today, so the change
has no ability to damage the 3%+ bucket that carries the book's entire profit.

**Why +1.25% and not the best-performing cell.** The counterfactual ordering is
+1.00 → +59.61, +1.25 → +67.37, **+1.50 → +23.07**, +2.00 → +43.73. That is
**not monotonic**, so the optimum is not identifiable from 79 trades and
picking the best cell would be the Run 4 error exactly. +1.25% is chosen
because it is where the trail's arming threshold sits — it is the edge of the
dead band by construction, not by search.

**Robustness — this is the part that is unusual:**

| test | result |
|---|---|
| full sample | +67.37 (+0.853/trade) |
| **drop top 2 trades** (gate criterion 4) | **+118.22 (+1.535/trade)** |
| first half (n=39) | +59.38 (+1.523/trade) |
| second half (n=40) | +7.99 (+0.200/trade) |

Dropping the top 2 trades makes it **bigger**. The benefit is spread across the
book rather than concentrated in a lucky tail. Sign holds in both halves —
though the second half is 7× weaker, which is an honest warning, not a
footnote.

**Implementation cost is real.** The bot submits Alpaca **bracket** orders,
which are full-quantity OCO. There is no partial-exit path in `orb_bot/`
(`grep` for scale/partial returns nothing relevant). This needs new order
plumbing, not a config flip.

**Per `PREREG-2026-09-22.md` this is a strategy change and goes to the sim
first, on an untouched window, once.** It does not qualify for the direct-to-
paper route that E1 and E3 get, because those are costs and measurements and
this is not.

## E6 — Breakeven stop  ⛔ do NOT deploy on this evidence

The obvious cheaper alternative — once the trade reaches +T%, move the stop to
cost with the existing `broker.replace_stop()` — **cannot be evaluated from
this data at all**, and I want that written down before it looks appealing.

| T | optimistic | pessimistic | actual |
|---|---|---|---|
| +0.75% | +301.19 | −188.71 | −52.65 |
| +1.00% | +207.12 | −279.92 | −52.65 |
| +1.25% | +173.62 | −311.48 | −52.65 |

*Optimistic* assumes no eventual winner is ever stopped out on a pullback.
*Pessimistic* assumes every trade that touches +T is stopped flat and all
upside is lost. The truth is somewhere between, and **the pessimistic case is
far worse than doing nothing.**

MFE/MAE summary statistics do not record whether the low came before or after
the high, so they cannot tell you which end of that range is real. Resolving it
needs bar-level replay in `backtest.py` against `.sweep_cache`. Until that is
run, the honest answer is *unknown*, not *+207*.

## E7 — `trade_features.csv`: header is wrong; the gap is probably a stale copy

**Corrected 2026-09-29, after checking.** My first reading of this was that the
feature logging had failed. The evidence does not support that:

- **`append_features()` never threw.** It is wrapped in `try/except Exception`
  and logs `Could not write trade features` on failure. That line appears in
  **zero** log files, across the whole trial.
- **The mtimes give it away.** `state/trade_history.csv` and every log through
  09-24 carry a **2026-09-24 21:02** mtime — they were synced down from the VPS
  in one go. `state/trade_features.csv` carries **2026-09-10 14:26**. It was
  simply not included in that sync.

So the 13-trades-vs-1-row gap is almost certainly **a stale local copy, not a
logging failure**. The VPS copy is very probably complete.

**Verify with one command on the VPS:** `wc -l state/trade_features.csv`.
If it has rows past 09-10, there is no bug and nothing to fix in `orb_bot/`.

**What IS a real defect, either way — the stale header.**
`append_features()` does `write_header = not FEATURES_FILE.exists()`, so the
header is written **once, at file creation**. This file predates v1.12, which
added `rel_volume_open` to `FEATURE_FIELDS`. Every row since has been written
with **18** fields under a **17**-field header.

Consequences: a plain `pd.read_csv` raises `ParserError` on the file, and a
tolerant parser silently shifts `spread_pct` into `rel_volume_open` and
`minutes_after_open` into `spread_pct` — wrong numbers under right-looking
column names, which is worse than a crash.

**Zero-risk fix: rewrite the header line of the file on the VPS.** The data
rows are already correct (`DictWriter` writes in `FEATURE_FIELDS` order); only
the header is wrong, so no code change and no deploy is needed:

```
date,entry_time_et,symbol,side,qty,entry_ref,or_pct,gap_pct,sentiment,n_articles,rel_volume,rel_volume_open,spread_pct,minutes_after_open,tp_pct,stop_pct,bias,added_intraday
```

Worth doing in `report.py` eventually so a fresh file can't drift again, but
that touches code shared with live and is not urgent.

## The 1.25 / 1.50 question — and why it is not answerable today

Short answer: **the data that would settle it is the data this project has
already ruled inadmissible.**

`stop_pct` is 1.5 and `trail_pct` is 1.25. The variable that tells you whether
a tighter stop would have helped is **MAE** — how far each trade went against
you before it worked. And the standing note from v1.18 is explicit: *do not
cite pre-v1.18 MAE figures in any stop-width argument*, because the IEX
minute-bar feed was producing impossible values (SNXX 2026-09-22 reported
MAE −13.69% against a −1.50% stop that never fired). Almost the whole trial
predates that guard.

Two further things worth knowing before anyone reaches for this lever:

1. **Stop width has never actually been swept.** Run 2 swept OR length and
   trail-on/off. 1.5 is inherited, not validated. That cuts both ways: it is
   not sacred, and there is also no evidence that moving it helps.
2. **The stop and the trail are coupled.** The dead band exists precisely
   because the trail (1.25) sits just under the stop (1.5) — the trail cannot
   arm until the trade is up roughly the same distance the stop is away.
   Narrowing the stop to 1.25 makes stop == trail: it cuts loss size by ~17%
   and raises stop-out frequency, and it does **not** close the dead band. You
   cannot reason about one number without the other, which is why the replay
   grids stop width and trail width side by side.

The honest position: 1.25-vs-1.50 is a parameter search, and this project is
0-for-7 on parameter searches. E5 is not — it adds a mechanism where none
exists. If the replay says stop width matters, that is a finding to holdout,
not a config change to make.

## The replay harness — `replay.py` (built 2026-09-29, offline-tested)

Walks the 1-minute bars of every trade in `state/trade_history.csv` forward
from entry and re-runs it under alternative exit rules. What it produces that
nothing here has had before:

- **True MFE/MAE with their ORDER** — the `mfe_first` column in
  `replay_trades.csv` says whether the high came before the low. That single
  column is what collapses E6's +207/−280 range into an answer.
- Grids for E6 (breakeven stop), E5 (scale-out), stop width and trail width,
  each reported under **both** fill assumptions.
- A **harness validation step that runs first**: it replays the live rules and
  compares with the P&L the bot actually booked. If those disagree badly,
  nothing else it prints should be believed. Run `--validate` before `--all`.

Known approximations, stated up front: trail arming is modelled as "peak
reached +arm%" rather than v1.15's exact "trailing from here still locks ≥
breakeven"; and within a bar the adverse extreme is assumed to come first, so
trailing exits read slightly optimistic and hard-stop exits slightly
pessimistic. The validation step is what surfaces how much that matters.

`.sweep_cache` turned out to hold pickled screener *candidates*, not bars, so
the replay fetches and caches its own into `.replay_cache/`. It reads keys from
`.env` exactly as the bot does — **no keys need to be pasted anywhere.**

Offline unit tests in `tests/test_replay.py` — 13 checks, no network: hard
stop, breakeven in isolation, trail-beats-breakeven, runner-not-clipped,
scale-out blending, wick fills under touch vs close, the thin-bar guard, and
the short side both ways. All pass; the file exits non-zero on failure.

**Run it (where your keys are — your machine or the VPS):**

```
python -u replay.py --validate
python -u replay.py --all | Tee-Object -FilePath replay_out.txt
```

Default feed is SIP, deliberately: the bot trades on IEX but the broker fills
against the consolidated tape, so SIP is the right ground truth for "would this
stop have filled". `--feed iex` shows what the bot itself could see, and the
gap between the two is worth a look on its own.

## Sequencing — why E3 goes first, and it is not close

E1 is already pre-registered and could go to paper tomorrow. It should not,
and the reason is that **E3 is the only perishable measurement in the queue.**

The paper-to-live fill haircut is measurable *only while both instances run the
same config*. The moment paper carries a change live does not, that comparison
is gone and cannot be reconstructed afterwards — you would be comparing two
different strategies on two different fill engines and unable to separate the
two effects. E1, by contrast, will still be there in three weeks.

`EXPERIMENTS.md` already says E3 is worth more than E1 and E2 combined. The
sequencing follows from that: **run paper identical to live until ~20
overlapping trades exist**, which at ~3 trades a day is roughly 7 sessions,
then start E1 on paper.

The prize is knowing how much of *every* paper number to believe — including
the E5 sim result, when it arrives. Getting that discount factor wrong makes
every future experiment unreadable.

## What I would do in order

1. **Tomorrow — change nothing on paper.** Run it identical to live and begin
   collecting the E3 overlap. On any day both instances trade the same symbol,
   record reference price, fill price and slippage from each.
2. **E7** — one `wc -l` on the VPS to confirm the feature file is intact, then
   rewrite its header line. No code change, no deploy.
3. **E1** — the entry-slippage band, once ~20 overlapping trades exist.
4. **E5** — write the bar-level replay, then the scale-out, criteria first.
5. **E6** — only after that replay says which end of its range is real.

Live stays frozen throughout.
