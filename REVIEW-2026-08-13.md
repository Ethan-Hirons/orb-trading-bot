nothing to diagnose in Aug 11–12; that is the system working as designed.

the fix remains untested rather than proven or disproven.

So the honest state is: the current build has **two trades of live evidence**,
and the single change it was built around is unvalidated. Changing parameters on
top of it now destroys the ability to attribute any future result to anything.

## 2. Honest read on the overall numbers

| Metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Net P&L, 55 trades | +5.06 |
| Profit factor | 1.012 |
| Expectancy | +0.09 / trade |
| Win rate | 41.8% |
| Median trade | **−2.72** |
| Median R | **−0.34R** |
| Net excluding top 3 winners | **−147.79** |

The whole result rests on three trades (+84.36, +34.39, +34.10). Profit factor
1.01 over 55 trades is statistically indistinguishable from zero edge — the
honest 95% interval around this spans clearly-losing to clearly-winning. **This
is not yet a system with a demonstrated edge, and it is not yet fundable by the
criteria in VALIDATION-GO-NOGO.md.** That is not a reaction to the last five
days; it was equally true on Aug 3 after the +127 day.

### On "minimize red days"

Worth being direct: red days cannot be targeted directly without damaging the
edge. Every method of reducing red-day *count* — widening stops, taking profits
early, cutting size after losses — trades a higher win rate for a worse
expectancy. Ethan's own Aug 11 sweep shows this cleanly: stop 3.0% gives the
**best** win rate (59.6%) and the **worst** P&L (−64.96). More green days, less
money.

This strategy family is inherently low-win-rate. The QQQ ORB paper reports a
**24% win rate** with positive expectancy, carried by rare large winners. Era A
here was 10 green / 6 red and netted +157. The red days are the cost of the
Aug-3-shaped days, not a defect to be engineered away.

## 3. The profit cap — you are mostly right, and one change is well-evidenced

Three separate things cap upside. Their current status:

1. **Per-trade take-profit — already off.** `tp_min_pct`/`tp_max_pct` are both
   25.0, an unreachable ceiling. Nothing to change.
2. **Daily profit breaker — this is the real cap, and it cost you.**
   `risk.daily_profit_target_pct: 6.0` fired on **Aug 3 at +6.14% and ended the
   single best day of the trial**. A profit breaker has no risk justification —
   it caps the right tail, which is the only part of this distribution that pays
   for everything else. Removing it is supported by both the literature (the
   paper's best variant is *no fixed target, exit at end of day*) and by your
   own single data point. **Recommended: remove.**
3. **EOD flatten — keep.** No overnight risk on a cash account, and the research
   strategies also exit at the close.

So: your instinct is right, it is already 2/3 implemented, and the remaining
piece is a one-line change with direct evidence behind it.

## 4. What the research actually says — and the one real gap it exposes

Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz (2024) tested this exact strategy shape across 7,000 US
stocks, 2016–2023. Their headline finding is uncomfortable and useful:

> **A plain ORB was weak.** Unfiltered: 29% total return over 8 years, Sharpe
> 0.48, **hit ratio 41.4%** — well below buy-and-hold. Adding one filter took it
> to 1,637% and Sharpe 2.81, hit ratio 48.4%.

That filter is **opening relative volume**: the stock's own first-5-minute volume
versus *its own* 14-day average first-5-minute volume. Their measured gradient:

| Opening relative volume | Avg result |
|---|---:|
| below 100% | −0.02R |
| above 100% | +0.08R |
| above 3000% | +0.38R |

**This bot does not compute that metric.** `screener.py:171` is:

```python
rel_volume = (actives.get(sym, 0.0) / max_vol) if max_vol else 0.0
```

That is today's raw volume divided by the *largest* volume in today's actives
list — a cross-sectional rank against other stocks, capped at 1.0. It measures
"is this a big-volume name" (mostly market cap), not "is this stock unusually
active *for itself*", which is the entire point. On top of that it is **0.0 for
45% of trades** (25/56), and it carries `weight_volume: 0.5` in the ranking.

The bot's own data agrees with the paper:

| | Trades | Net | Avg |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| rel_volume == 0 | 23 | **−83.82** | −3.64 |
| rel_volume > 0 | 30 | +36.94 | +1.23 |

And the tell: this bot's hit ratio is **41.8%** — almost exactly the paper's
*unfiltered* variant (41.4%), not the filtered one (48.4%). The selection is
currently gap + news sentiment, which is a proxy for "something happened", where
the research says the thing that predicts follow-through is *measured abnormal
participation*.

**This is the highest-value change available and it is not a parameter tweak.**

### Second finding: fixed % stops vs volatility-scaled stops

The paper sizes its stop at **10% of 14-day ATR** — scaled to each stock's own
volatility. This bot uses a flat 1.5% of price for every name. That means the
stop is very tight on a name with a 4% opening range and loose on a 1% name. The
trade data splits accordingly:

| Opening range width | Trades | Net |
|---|---:|---:|
| <1% | 6 | −2.31 |
| 1–2% | 7 | **+19.57** |
| 2–4% | 9 | −2.85 |
| **4%+** | **29** | **−35.77** |

The Aug 11 sweep settled 1.5 vs 2.0 vs 2.5 vs 3.0 — but every row was a *fixed*
stop. Volatility-scaled stops are an axis that has never been swept, and it is
what the literature uses.

### Third: opening range length

The paper found the 5-minute range materially beat longer ones (1,637% vs 272%
at 15 min, on identical rules). This bot uses `opening_range_minutes: 15`.
Cheap to sweep.

### Where the literature does *not* help

Both papers assume simplified execution and the QQQ one assumes **zero
slippage**; neither has a clean out-of-sample period; the extreme 9,350% variant
is an acknowledged parameter search. Their absolute returns are not achievable
claims. Treat them as a source of *structural* ideas — relative volume, ATR-scaled
stops, no profit target — not as target numbers.

## 6. Would an agent alongside the bot help?

**In the trading loop: no.** Concretely bad idea here:

- Latency — the 30s poll is already a known problem; an LLM call is seconds more.
- Non-determinism — you cannot sweep or backtest a component that answers
  differently each time, so you lose the only tool that has been settling
  arguments in this project.
- Attribution — this system's failures to date have been *silent engineering
  failures* (`replace_order` swallowed by `except: pass`, an unverified
  `exit_position`). Adding an unauditable decision-maker to a codebase with that
  failure history makes the next bug much harder to find.

**Around the loop: yes, and there's an obvious concrete win.** The trailing stop
was broken for four trading days and cost roughly −130. The daily recap ran
through all four of those days and did not catch it, because it only reported
what happened, not what *should have been impossible*. Invariant checks would
have caught it on Aug 4:

- any position whose MFE exceeded `trail_pct` that still exited at the full
  initial stop → the trail did not fire
- a trail configured on but zero `TRAIL ACTIVATED` lines across a day with a
  qualifying position
- realized loss materially exceeding the intended stop (this would have flagged
  AHCO at −5.39% vs a −1.50% stop on day one)
- any position open at the next session's start
- `trade_history.csv` row count not matching entry/exit pairs in the log

That is the single highest-value automation available: not a smarter trader, a
loud auditor. Recommended as the next change to the recap task.

**Multiple bots: right idea, wrong time.** Uncorrelated strategies genuinely do
smooth an equity curve — that is the actual, non-destructive answer to "too many
red days", and it is what the paper means by low beta. But it only works with
strategies that each have an edge. Right now there is one strategy with an
unproven edge and 55 trades. Splitting attention and capital across two gives
~27 trades each, doubles the surface area for silent bugs, and makes both
unvalidatable. Correct order: get one strategy past its own go/no-go criteria,
then add a second chosen for low correlation to it.

## 7. Recommended sequence

**Now (does not disturb validation):**

1. Remove `daily_profit_target_pct` — the one change with direct evidence and no
   risk cost. Keep `daily_max_loss_pct: 5.0` and the EOD flatten.
2. Add the invariant checks above to the daily recap.
3. Make sure the bot starts *every* trading day. Aug 7 and Aug 10 have no logs at
   all — that is two lost validation days out of a week, and the go/no-go
   checklist requires a full one.

**Next, in the sweep — not live:**

4. Implement the paper's relative volume properly (own first-5-min volume vs own
   14-day average) and sweep it as a hard filter. Highest expected value by a
   wide margin.
5. Sweep an ATR-scaled stop against the current fixed 1.5%.
6. Sweep `opening_range_minutes: 5` against 15.
7. Sweep the entry window at 60 min against the current 180.

**Then:** rerun the paper week on whatever wins, and hold to the funding
criteria already written in VALIDATION-GO-NOGO.md — including at least one
observed `TRAIL ACTIVATED` → `TRAIL EXIT` cycle, which still has not happened.

One thing worth holding onto: on Aug 3 this bot made +127 in a day and the
config stopped it. The problem has never been that the good days aren't there.
