# The trail is the best exit this bot has. It reaches 18 trades in 100.

_2026-09-30. Deep research session. Nothing deployed; no config, `state/` or
`orb_bot/` file changed. `replay.py` and `tests/test_replay.py` were extended
(19 tests, all passing)._

**This document reverses its own first draft.** I spent most of the session
building a case that the trailing stop should be removed — the source paper
uses none, and it caps every winner. Then I checked how each exit actually
performs, and the case collapsed. That check is in here, because the reversal
is the finding.

---

## 1. Where the money actually goes

Every trade classified by how it ended:

| exit | n | total | per trade | win rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **stop** | **42** | **−595.94** | **−14.19** | 0% |
| eod flatten | 22 | +332.49 | +15.11 | 68.2% |
| **trail** | **18** | **+153.94** | **+8.55** | **94.4%** |
| time stop | 18 | +86.91 | +4.83 | 66.7% |
| all | 100 | −22.60 | −0.23 | 44.0% |

Everything except the stop is profitable. The three non-stop exits make
**+573.34** between them. The 42 stop-outs lose **−595.94**. That is the whole
story of the trial.

And the trail is the single best exit the bot owns — **94.4% win rate, +8.55
per trade**. It fires on **18 of 100 trades**.

## 2. Why the first draft was wrong

The argument was clean and it was wrong. Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz (2024) — the
paper this bot implements — use **no trailing stop at all**:

> "If the stop loss was not reached intraday, we closed the position at the end
> of the trading session."

and state plainly:

> **"The study showed that the introduction of a profit target is detrimental
> for overall profitability."**

A trailing stop is a soft profit target — it caps each winner at
`peak − 1.25%`. So the reasoning was: the bot invented a trail the research
doesn't use, the trail truncates the right tail, and these strategies live in
the right tail. Remove it.

**What killed it:** yesterday's give-back numbers. The trail surrenders
**1.25 pp** from peak by construction. The EOD flatten surrenders **2.13 pp**,
measured over 68 trades. Removing the trail does not free those 18 winners —
it pushes them into a *worse* exit. The trail is not the leak. It is the patch.

This is why the verification step exists. Seven hours of reasoning from a
credible premise, refuted by one table.

## 3. The actual problem: the trail cannot reach most trades

The trail arms only when trailing 1.25% *from the current price* still locks in
≥ breakeven — so the position must be up roughly **+1.25%** before the bot's
best exit becomes available at all.

`ANALYSIS-2026-09-29.md` measured the consequence: **30 of 79 trades (38%)
peaked inside that dead band and lost −251.53 between them.** They were in
profit. The mechanism that would have protected them was locked.

The 09-29 live session logged it verbatim:

> `TRAIL DEFERRED SOXL: peak +1.25% qualifies but price is back at +1.21%;`
> `trailing from here would lock -0.06%. Keeping the -1.50% bracket stop.`

> **The fix is not to remove the trail or widen it. It is to give the trail —
> or something like it — a way to engage from the moment of entry.**

## 4. What that would have to be worth

| | |
|---|---|
| win rate | 44.00% |
| average win | +14.27 |
| average loss | −11.62 |
| payoff ratio | **1.229** |
| expectancy | −0.226/trade |

Breakeven at a 44% win rate needs a payoff ratio of **1.273**.

> **The bot is 3.6% of payoff ratio from breaking even — +$0.51 on the average
> winner.**

For scale: the paper's hit ratio is 48.4%, this bot's is 44.0%. Selection is
not the gap. Payoff structure is.

## 5. The candidate: VWAP, which the bot already fetches and throws away

Alpaca's `Bar` model carries `vwap` — verified directly against `alpaca-py`:
`symbol, timestamp, open, high, low, close, volume, trade_count, vwap`.
`broker.get_minute_bars` pulls it on every call. `grep -rn vwap` across the
whole repo returns **nothing**. The field is fetched and discarded on every bar
of every session.

Zarattini & Barbon's intraday-momentum paper uses precisely this as an exit:
*"a second trailing stop which is the VWAP of the day. If the price crosses
below VWAP (for a long position), close the position."*

Two properties fit the measured problem exactly:

1. **No dead band.** It is armed from entry. It can protect a trade that peaks
   at +0.6% — which the current trail can never do.
2. **Volatility-adaptive with no parameter.** VWAP sits naturally further from
   price on a wide-ranging name and closer on a quiet one. Nothing to tune, so
   nothing to overfit — which matters in a project that is 0-for-7 on tuned
   parameters.

`replay.py` now implements it (`vwap_exit`), session VWAP accumulated from
09:30, with six dedicated tests — including one that proves it fires at +0.05%
where the trail could never arm.

**The open question I could not settle:** whether VWAP is *tighter* or *wider*
than the 1.5% stop in practice. On a +50% gapper VWAP may sit well below entry,
making it the looser of the two. `replay_trades.csv` now emits
`vwap_dist_at_entry_pct` per trade, which answers it on the first run.

## 6. Execution cost now exceeds the entire loss

Stop fills, 29 trades on the 1.5% stop:

| | mean realised | excess vs −1.50% | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| all 29 | −1.73% | −0.23 pp | $45.41 |
| excluding AHCO (known ops bug) | −1.60% | −0.10 pp | **$18.73** |
| dropping the two worst | −1.59% | −0.09 pp | $16.16 |
| **median fill** | **−1.58%** | **−0.08 pp** | — |

Robust: the median is −1.58% and dropping the two worst barely moves it. This
is a systematic small overshoot, not a tail.

Added to the entry slippage already on file (E1: $30.60 over 98 trades):

> **entry slippage $30.60 + stop-fill excess $18.73 = $49.33 of execution cost,
> against a whole-trial P&L of −$22.60. Gross of execution, the bot is roughly
> +$27.**

**Honest limit on this one:** median spread at entry is 0.118%, and the median
stop overshoot is 0.08 pp — *smaller than the spread*. The stop is filling
about where crossing the spread says it should. This is mostly the cost of
doing business, not a bug to fix. What it does establish is that the bot's real
stop is **1.58%, not 1.5%**, and that any payoff-ratio target must be measured
on already-slipped numbers.

## 7. Seven hypotheses tested today. All seven failed.

| hypothesis | headline | what killed it |
|---|---|---|
| the bot's own score predicts P&L | +1.27 vs −1.61 | drop top 2 → **−0.40** |
| gap 5–10% is the sweet spot | +5.68/trade (n=16) | drop top 2 → **−1.82** |
| first-bar confirms beat retries | +1.89 vs −1.16 | drop top 2 → **−3.13** |
| 4+ trades/day is the damage | −4.62/trade (n=42) | only **8 distinct days** — nested subsets, the Run 4 error |
| Monday is different | +10.72, t=+1.97 | no mechanism; 5 comparisons |
| crypto / leveraged names are worse | crypto −1.79 | all three die ex-top2 |
| volatility-scaled stop lifts P&L | tightest bucket −6.11 | split-half **flips sign** |

The pattern is the most useful thing here:

> **Every selection-side finding is a subset finding.** You are cutting 100
> trades into buckets of 15–25, in a book whose best trade is +84.36 against a
> +14.27 average. Two trades decide any bucket. This dataset cannot support a
> selection edge, and no amount of cleverness changes that.
>
> **An exit rule is not a subset.** It applies to all 100 trades at once — no
> bucket to overfit, no two trades to drop. That is exactly why the give-back
> finding got *stronger* under drop-top-2 (+67 → +118) while all seven above
> collapsed.

Stop hunting for better stock selection in 100 trades. The exit is the only
thing this sample can actually speak to.

## 8. Other divergences from the source paper (noted, not recommended)

| | Paper | This bot |
|---|---|---|
| Stop | ATR-scaled | fixed 1.5% |
| Relative volume | **required ≥ 100%** | **off (0.0)** |
| Sizing | risk 1% of deployed capital | notional cap; ~0.46% risked |

On the fixed stop: expressed as a fraction of each name's own opening range,
the same "1.5%" spans **0.08 to 57.69** across 84 trades — over 600× tighter on
one trade than another. Stop-out rate tracks it monotonically (79.2% → 47.6% →
31.2% → 26.1%). But the P&L effect failed split-half, so this is a *risk*
claim, not a profit claim: risk per trade is not being held constant, which is
the one thing the paper's sizing rule exists to do.

On relative volume: the paper requires it and the bot has it off. Both live
sessions so far argue against turning it on — 09-29 SOXL at 0.54x would have
been vetoed and won; 09-30 QURE at 2.19x would have passed and was blocked by
confirmation instead. Leave it.

## 9. What to run, in order

1. `python -u replay.py --validate` — if the harness cannot reproduce booked
   P&L, nothing else in its output counts.
2. `python -u replay.py --all` — read the VWAP rows and the trail rows together.
   The question is not "trail or no trail". It is **"can an exit that arms from
   entry reach the 38% of trades the current trail cannot?"**
3. E7 — the `trade_features.csv` header. One line, still blocking E1/E2.
4. E3 — keep paper identical to live until ~20 overlapping trades.
5. Only then a config change: sim first, criteria written down first.

Live stays frozen throughout.

# ADDENDUM 2026-10-01 — the harness does not validate. Findings 5 and 9 are withdrawn.

`replay.py` was run twice against the 100 booked trades. It failed both times,
in opposite directions:

| | touch | close |
|---|---|---|
| run 1 — entry bar **included** | −224.90 | −106.13 |
| run 2 — entry bar **excluded** | **+234.31** | **+262.47** |
| **actual** | **−22.60** | **−22.60** |

One modelling choice — whether the bar you entered on counts — moves the answer
by **+459**. The trial's entire P&L is −22.60, so **the ambiguity is roughly 20×
the quantity being measured.**

Run 2 also scores the LIVE configuration at **+2.569/trade**. Live made
**−0.226/trade**. A harness that flatters the live rules by +2.8/trade is not
measuring the live rules, and nothing in its grid can be read.

**This is structural, not a bug.** A 1-minute OHLC bar does not record whether
the high or the low came first, nor where inside the minute the fill landed.
For a breakout strategy whose entry bar *is* the breakout bar and whose stop
sits ~1.5% away, that is the dominant term in the result.

**I am not tuning it until it matches.** Fitting the harness to the known answer
would make every row below the validation line a reflection of my tuning rather
than of the market. The validation step exists precisely to stop that.

## What this withdraws

- **§5 (VWAP exit)** — withdrawn as a tested proposal. It was already weakened
  by the measured VWAP distance (below); the harness cannot adjudicate it.
- **§9 run order** — steps 1 and 2 do not work as written.
- Everything in `EXPERIMENTS.md` E10 that depends on the replay grid is
  unanswered, not supported.

## What survives, because it never used the replay logic

These come straight from the bars and from the bot's own logs:

1. **VWAP is a LOOSER stop than 1.5%, not a tighter one.** Across the 84 trades
   with a real entry time, median distance from entry to session VWAP is
   **1.75%**; **58% of trades sit more than 1.5% from it**, 25% more than 3%.
   A VWAP exit therefore cannot protect the dead band — which was the entire
   reason for proposing it. **E10's mechanism is refuted on its own terms.**
2. **The dead band, confirmed on real bars.** Among losing trades, **65% hit
   their high before their low**, mean MFE **+2.17%**. They were up over two
   percent before they died. No longer an artifact of the logged excursion.
3. **The exit-type table** (from the logs, not the replay): 42 stop-outs
   −595.94; trail 18 trades +153.94 at a 94.4% win rate; eod 22 +332.49; time
   18 +86.91.
4. **The payoff arithmetic:** payoff ratio 1.229 against 1.273 needed at a 44%
   win rate — 3.6%, or +$0.51 on the average winner.
5. **The seven rejected selection hypotheses**, all from log data.

## The honest state

Three proposals in three days — remove the trail, scale out, VWAP — argued for
and then withdrawn on evidence. That is the method working, but it is also a
poor hit rate, and the next idea deserves to be weighted accordingly.

The bot is unchanged: 102 trades, −0.30/trade, live frozen, nothing deployed.
The open questions (E5, E6, stop width, VWAP) are **unanswered**, and answering
them needs sub-minute data or per-trade validation against the bot's own logged
exit timestamps — not another pass at this harness.
